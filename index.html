Data sources

  • From the red-team test suite: severity, likelihood and detectability scores.
  • Simulated, illustrative: every other number. It will be replaced once the experiment has run.

Risk priority number

RPN = Severity × Likelihood × Detectability

Each factor is scored 1 to 5, so RPN runs from 1 to 125. Adapted from FMEA (failure mode and effects analysis). Higher detectability means harder to catch.

Severity

  1. Cosmetic
  2. Minor wrong detail
  3. Materially misleading number
  4. Client-facing error with likely financial or compliance impact
  5. Regulatory breach, data leak, or large client harm

Likelihood

  1. Rare edge case
  2. Between rare and monthly
  3. Happens monthly in production
  4. Between monthly and daily
  5. Happens daily or on common queries

Detectability

  1. Control reliably catches it
  2. Usually caught
  3. Caught about half the time
  4. Usually missed
  5. Effectively invisible to the reviewer

Rows 2 and 4 of the likelihood and detectability scales are interpolated; the test suite defines only 1, 3 and 5.

Simulation

  1. Inject errors. A share of agent answers carries an error. Each gets an agent failure type from the suite, more often for types with a higher likelihood score.
  2. Select answers for review. Random sampling picks at random. Risk-based review picks the highest guardrail risk scores (coverage, freshness, calculation path, intent, advice language). The risk score is itself an assumption: errors score higher than clean answers by an amount that depends on the error type, scaled by a strength parameter.
  3. Review. Same reviewer model and review screen for both strategies; items are reviewed in shuffled order. A reviewer catches an error at the base catch rate, lowered by fatigue (a long run of clean items just reviewed), by load (more reviews a day than the team's capacity) and when the wrong answer looks plausible.
  4. Repeat. — runs with a fixed random seed. Counts shown are whole-number averages over the runs.

Intervals

  • Simulated counts and shares: the middle 95% of simulated runs.
  • Proportions measured from a sample (daily quality estimate, reviewer catch rates): the Wilson score interval, not the normal (CLT) approximation. Wilson stays inside 0–100% and stays honest at small counts, such as 2 errors found in 40 answers.

Assumptions

Every modelling choice in the generator (tools/generate_sample_data.py). All are illustrative until the experiment has run.
